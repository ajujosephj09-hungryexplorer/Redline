# Real People Describing Pain from Contract Terms They Did Not Understand or Notice

Search count used: 12 | Pages read: 9 | Findings: 8

---

**Quote:** "the interest rate was higher than they told me over the phone and the total payment to them was going to be well over $200,000"
**Source:** https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/
**Contract type:** Student loan / financing agreement (flight school)
**What went wrong:** A prospective pilot was verbally quoted a lower interest rate than what appeared in the final loan paperwork. The total repayment amount ballooned to over $200,000. The person canceled their career plans entirely after catching the discrepancy before signing.

**Quote:** "it effectively stated I'd be relinquishing my unlimited data and going to a 2 gig plan. The price was the same – 400/5 lines, but the data went from unlimited to 2 gigs"
**Source:** https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/
**Contract type:** Telecommunications service renewal (Verizon Wireless)
**What went wrong:** A customer nearly agreed to a phone plan renewal that kept the same monthly price but cut their data from unlimited down to 2GB. The change was buried in the renewal contract. They caught it only by reading the document carefully before signing.

**Quote:** "Loan was a $250,000 at 25%!"
**Source:** https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/
**Contract type:** Mortgage / home loan
**What went wrong:** A borrower nearly signed a home loan at a 25% interest rate — a predatory rate buried in the contract documents. The quote implies the person did not notice the rate until reading (or almost not reading) the final paperwork.

**Quote:** "the 'small fee' is 18% of the total life insurance claim! 180 000 for filling out the forms on 1 million claim!"
**Source:** https://humansoftumblr.com/always-read-the-contract-17-redditors-share-horror-stories-about-the-fine-print/
**Contract type:** Funeral services agreement
**What went wrong:** A bereaved family signed an agreement with a funeral home that included a fee described verbally as "small." The contract defined that fee as 18% of the total life insurance claim — amounting to $180,000 on a $1 million policy — simply for the funeral home filling out claim paperwork. The clause was not clearly explained upfront.

---

**Quote:** "To be eligible for subsequent corrective surgeries— the participant must see an optometrist every year after surgery."
**Source:** https://creators.yahoo.com/lifestyle/story/the-most-deceptive-fine-print-ive-ever-seen-in-a-contract-173526785.html
**Contract type:** Medical procedure warranty agreement (LASIK surgery)
**What went wrong:** A patient paid $1,200 for a lifetime warranty on LASIK corrective surgery but did not notice a buried eligibility condition requiring an annual optometrist visit. Missing even one yearly appointment would void the warranty entirely, meaning the $1,200 warranty payment provided no protection if any single annual visit was skipped.

---

**Quote:** "I didn't have a clause on late payment in my contract"
**Source:** https://blog.zoho.com/index.php/sign/blog/why-you-need-to-have-a-freelance-contract-agreement.html (freelancer Dana Nicole quoted)
**Contract type:** Freelance services contract
**What went wrong:** Freelancer Dana Nicole did not include a late payment penalty clause in her client contracts. Without that provision, she had no legal mechanism to enforce penalties when clients paid late repeatedly. She was unable to recoup losses from chronic late payment, and the client relationship was damaged by the disputes that followed.

**Quote:** "The mere fact that one does not read a contract which he has signed is not grounds to invalidate the writing." [court ruling against employee] — and separately, the employee "did not sign an arbitration agreement" and contended the agreement was not binding because he was "unaware" of its existence.
**Source:** https://hrdailyadvisor.hci.org/2021/06/15/employee-unaware-of-signed-arbitration-agreement-compelled-to-arbitrate/
**Contract type:** Employment agreement (mandatory arbitration clause)
**What went wrong:** A supermarket clerk signed employment onboarding paperwork that included two separate arbitration agreements — both clearly titled "arbitration agreement" — without reading them. When he later filed an FMLA retaliation lawsuit in federal court, his employer invoked the arbitration clause. The court dismissed his federal case and compelled arbitration, eliminating his ability to pursue litigation. His claim of unawareness was rejected.

**Quote:** "This clause effectively put some freelancers out of work, because they simply did not know that the non-compete clause they had signed was illegal."
**Source:** https://colleendoran.substack.com/p/the-none-compete-clause
**Contract type:** Freelance services contract (non-compete clause)
**What went wrong:** A client presented a young freelance creator with a contract containing a non-compete clause spanning 7 years and restricting the freelancer from working on "anything that was in the same style as anything he had ever published" — regardless of whether they had actually worked on those projects. The clause also prohibited public appearances, teaching, and discussing their work. Freelancers who signed similar clauses without legal review effectively ceased working, not knowing the restriction was unenforceable.
